What's on the shelf

Walk into any Malaysian pharmacy or beauty store and the choice is overwhelming. To see what is on offer, we start with 8,494 products listed by Sephora, a global beauty retailer that also has stores in Malaysia.

A crowded shelf

8,494 products from 304 brands, and no single brand holds more than 8% of any category. Skincare, the focus of this page, is shown in purple.

1Where our beauty comes from

Most of the skincare on Malaysian shelves is made abroad. Each amber line traces where it was shipped from; thicker lines carry more value.

Who sells to us

Our beauty imports doubled in ten years. Singapore rose from 6th to 1st supplier, while Japan slipped from 4th to 7th.

And who we sell to

Malaysia sold US$173 M of make-up and skincare to 187 countries in 2024, and 57% went to our ASEAN neighbours.

2What it costs us at home

Personal-care prices, including salon services, have climbed in every state, but not at the same pace. Compared with Malaysia overall (+19.5% in ten years), darker teal states rose faster and lighter states more slowly.

Products cheaper, salons pricier

Compared with 2010 prices, beauty products (thick line) now cost 19% more, less than the 38% rise in prices overall. Salon and grooming services cost 57% more.

3Does paying more buy better skincare?

Products may be getting cheaper, but prices on the shelf still vary enormously. Is the expensive bottle worth it? We compared prices with ratings from over a million customer reviews.

4What the label promises

Brands love to advertise star ingredients. These are the ones each product type puts on its label most often.

How to read it

Darker brick-red squares mean more products of that type advertise it. Hyaluronic acid leads (16% of skincare), while oil-free claims are rare (4%).

Reading the labels

Only about 1 in 9 skincare products is fragrance-free.

5Made for Malaysian skin?

A product rated 4.5★ overall can still disappoint you if your skin is different from the reviewers'. Here we split the reviews by the skin type reviewers reported.

Oily vs dry skin

Humid weather means many Malaysians have oily or combination skin. Most products please oily skin and dry skin alike, but a few split them sharply.

Looking for a sunscreen that suits oily skin? Each line is one product. Follow the purple line for a good all-rounder.

Three things to remember at the beauty counter

1

A higher price doesn't buy a better rating

Skincare under US$25 averages 4.17★; skincare over US$250 averages 4.24★. Compare reviews and the ingredients you need before paying more.

2

Read reviews from people with your skin type

Most products please oily and dry skin alike, but a few split them by more than a star, especially sunscreens. The "Best for Oily" label barely changes how oily skin rates a product.

3

The label advertises; the ingredient list tells

Only 12% of skincare is fragrance-free and just 4% claims to be oil-free. If your skin is sensitive or oily, check the full ingredient list, not just the front of the box.

These tips come from US Sephora products and reviews, so treat them as a guide rather than advice for your own skin.
