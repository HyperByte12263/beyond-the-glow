What's on the shelf

Malaysian beauty shelves are packed. To see what is on offer, we start with 8,494 products from Sephora, a global retailer with stores in Malaysia.

A crowded shelf

304 brands share the shelf, and none holds more than 8% of any category. Skincare, this page's focus, is purple.

1Where our beauty comes from

Most skincare on Malaysian shelves is made abroad. Each amber line traces a supplier; thicker lines carry more value.

Who sells to us

Imports doubled in ten years, to US$624 million. Singapore climbed from 6th to 1st supplier; Japan slid from 4th to 7th.

And who we sell to

Malaysia sells too: US$173 million in 2024, to 187 countries. 57% went to our South-East Asian neighbours.

2What it costs us at home

Personal-care prices rose in every state, but not equally. Against Malaysia's 19.5% rise since 2016, darker teal states rose faster.

Products cheaper, salons pricier

Since 2010, beauty products (thick line) rose 19%, half the 38% rise in overall prices. Salon and grooming services rose 57%.

3Does paying more buy better skincare?

Products got relatively cheaper, yet shelf prices still vary hugely. Is the expensive bottle worth it? We checked over a million reviews.

The price spread

A typical moisturiser costs US$58. The dearest costs US$400, almost seven times as much.

If pricier products were better, the dots would climb to the top right. They don't. Drag across the chart to select products; they turn purple.

4What the label promises

Brands love to advertise star ingredients. Here is what each product type puts on its label.

How to read it

Darker brick-red squares mean more products advertise it. Hyaluronic acid leads (16% of skincare); oil-free claims are rare (4%).

Reading the labels

Only about 1 in 9 skincare products is fragrance-free, worth checking if your skin is sensitive.

5Made for Malaysian skin?

A product rated 4.5★ can still disappoint if your skin differs from the reviewers'. So we split reviews by reported skin type.

Oily versus dry skin

Humid weather leaves many Malaysians with oily or combination skin. Most products please oily skin and dry skin alike, but some, especially sunscreens, split them by up to 1.5 stars.

Need a sunscreen for oily skin? Each line is one product. The purple line is a cheap, well-rated all-rounder.

Three things to remember at the beauty counter

1

A higher price doesn't buy a better rating

Skincare under US$25 averages 4.17★; over US$250, 4.24★. Check reviews and ingredients before paying more.

2

Read reviews from people with your skin type

Most products suit oily and dry skin alike, but some sunscreens split them by up to 1.5 stars. Labels like "Best for Oily" barely help.

3

The label advertises; the ingredient list tells

Only 12% of skincare is fragrance-free and 4% oil-free. Check the full ingredient list, not just the box front.

These tips come from US Sephora products and reviews, so treat them as a guide rather than advice for your own skin.
